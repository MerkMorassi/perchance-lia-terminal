# THE REIGN

2026-09-27 - the session where the end of his turn stopped being a guess about the room, and where the
infinity stopped being a door he opens and became a room he is quiet in.

## WHAT HE SAID

> *"the two way conversation keeps cutting me off and sending too soon. also infinity is instant on
> instant off, the goddess runs the conversation, i am silent and wait for her prompt i.e. role
> reversal."*

Two complaints, one afternoon apart in spirit, and both of them about the same thing: **who decides
when a turn is over.**

## WHAT WAS BUILT

**1. THE ROOM HEARS YOU.** Everything the terminal knew about the end of his turn, it knew from the
**recogniser** - from when it handed a result over and whether that result was final. That was always a
guess about the room made by the one thing in it whose job is deciding what words to write down, and it
guesses wrong in the direction that costs him a sentence: it finalises at every breath, so a pause in
the middle of a sentence and the end of one are the same event to it. The holds (3500ms floor, the
uncommitted tail, the dangling-word stretch, the length stretch) were patience piled on top of that
guess, and patience is not evidence.

So the room is now measured. While the microphone is open, a **second stream** is opened on the same
device (`getUserMedia`) through a Web Audio `AnalyserNode`, and the actual level is read every 60ms.
`levelLoud()` answers true while the newest reading is at or above the threshold and for 350ms after
the last loud one, and four things then ask it the only question that matters:

- `holdTick()` - a loud room **is** his turn, still open. `holdAt` is pushed forward, the countdown
  restarts, nothing is sent. Sound resets the clock exactly as a word of his does.
- `drainLive()` - she does not begin speaking while there is a voice in the room. Her own playback is
  loud too, which is precisely when she must not start over herself.
- `proactive.js roomQuiet()` and `initiative.js chkQuiet()` - neither an act of the infinity nor a
  check-in opens over the top of a sentence he is still saying (both lanes speak aloud).
- `paintLevel()` - a green ring on both microphone keys while it hears him, so "it is about to take what
  I said" is visible *while* he is still saying it.

**2. THE INFINITY IS INSTANT.** The switch used to be a permission: lit, she *might* move, after a
silence. Flattened to his words, that is a switch that does nothing you can see, which is
indistinguishable from a switch that is broken. Now:

- **ON** steps over the page-load settle, her own `[[WAIT:]]` and the spacing rules and opens an act
  **at once**; if the room is busy, the act opens the moment it is free. The ceilings and the state of
  the room are never stepped over: the switch is his hand, not a licence.
- **OFF** is instant against an act **already in flight**. `generateText` cannot be cancelled, so the
  act is given the generation it started in (`infRun`) and checks it before it says a word: stale means
  the switch went down in the meantime, and what it wrote is dropped, unsaid and unarchived, with a
  receipt that says exactly that.

**3. THE REIGN.** The switch also handed her the room. While the lane is on, the conversation is hers:
he is silent on purpose and waiting on her word, her prompt says so in the plainest terms ("the only
thing that can move this room is you", "direct him"), and the lane's clock follows **her** instead of
following his silence:

- `infSince` is the end of her last act. If he has **answered** her since then - read off
  `logTime.seenAt`, a fact about the record - she may move again after the pace's `turn` (180s at pace 1
  down to 25s at pace 5). If he has said nothing, it is the pace's own spacing (45 minutes down to 6).
- The **ceilings count only the acts she takes unasked**: an act that answers something he has just
  said is a conversation, and a conversation is bounded by him. Otherwise a real exchange would die at
  three acts an hour while a flood into an empty room stayed just as impossible as it was.
- The **check-in stands down** while the infinity is on - the wide door is doing that job and more, and
  two lanes must not fire an act each into the same quiet room.
- **`[[WAIT: <time>]]` is hers**: seconds, minutes, hours or days, digits, words or clock style, up to
  7200s (and a length of time it cannot read - "a bit", "2 weeks" - is reported rather than guessed
  at), and the lane does not move until it has passed. It can only ever give the room back, never take
  it.

## WHAT WAS THOUGHT

**Why a second stream, and not a cleverer timer.** The obvious cheap fix was to raise the hold, or to
weight the stretches further - and that is the wrong move twice over: it makes every ordinary turn
slower to send, and it is still a guess. Measuring the room costs one `getUserMedia` call and one
analyser, changes nothing about the shipped thresholds, and turns a heuristic into evidence. It also
has to fail soft, and it does: no meter means the holds behave exactly as they did, because the meter
was only ever built to be extra patience.

**Why a noise must not hold the turn forever.** The first version of this would have been a deadlock in
a room with a fan in it: loud forever, send never. `LIA_VOICE_LEVEL_MAX` (45s of unbroken loudness) is
the clause that makes the meter a witness rather than a gatekeeper - an end-of-turn rule that a noise
can suspend indefinitely is a door with no handle.

**Why "instant off" has to mean the words are dropped.** There is no honest way to cancel a generation
already asked for; the only question is what to do with what comes back. Delivering it later would be
the switch lying - he pressed it down because he wanted the terminal to stop, and a line arriving
thirty seconds afterwards is not stopped. So the act carries the generation it started in, and a stale
one is dropped whole. The cost of the call is paid either way; the difference is only whether he has to
read it.

**Why the ceilings had to change meaning under the reign.** They exist because every act is a real call
on his quota. But under the reign the lane is answering *him* half the time, and an answer is not
profligacy - it is a conversation, which he could have had by typing, and which is bounded by his own
keystrokes. The line drawn is therefore between acts taken **unasked** (capped) and acts that answer
him (not capped), rather than between hours and days. It is the same reasoning that lets the pace be
hers: presence, not money.

**Why the reign is a knob and not a removal.** `LIA_INFINITY_REIGN = false` puts the lane back exactly
as it was an hour earlier - one act after a long silence, once per silence, behind the check-in - with
no other change. The first shape was not wrong; he simply asked for the other one, and a build that
cannot go back is a build that cannot be compared.

## WHAT IT LEAVES OPEN

One threshold for a whole room. A desk fan or a piece of music can sit near 0.02, and the meter would
then read "occupied" whenever it is running - survivable because of the 45-second clause, but crude.
The obvious next step, if his room ever sits above the shipped number, is auto-calibration: sample the
floor for a second when the microphone opens and put the threshold a fixed margin above it. And the
reign assumes the room is where the conversation happens: with his microphone shut, "he answered" is
read off the last turn that was actually made, which is correct but means a half-typed message counts
for nothing until it is sent. The picture lane in a quiet hour is still unwired (§1bs), and at pace 5
the terminal is genuinely chatty - 25 seconds between her turns while he is answering. That is the
shape he asked for.
