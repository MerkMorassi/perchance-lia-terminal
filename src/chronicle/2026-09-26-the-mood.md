# THE MOOD - A COLOUR YOU CAN PRESS, AND A "+" FOR WRITING YOUR OWN

2026-09-26, the session after THE TANDEM - the one in which the SYSTEM panel stopped being only a list
of blocks and got the lightest thing it can hold: a MOOD, a colour on how she speaks right now, set by
a chip, handed to her as a block of her own prompt, and written on the fly by either of them.

## WHAT HE SAID

Three sentences, and the second one is the specification:

> *"Add a MOOD MODIFIER in SYSTEM. Also look at how we implement similar ideas in the PENCIL icon that
> determine the response. Also, I need that '+' button reinstated so that I/WE can create new ones on
> the fly."*

Read together: a modifier he can press, shaped like the thing that already presses modifiers in this
terminal, and a way for BOTH of them to write a new one without anyone editing a file.

## WHAT THE PENCIL HAD THAT THE PROMPT DID NOT

The draft panel (`src/js/draft.js`) has been the answer to "how do we let one press change the
response" since the pencil was built: a row of chips - FIX, WORK, CHAT, PLAY, SHORT, VEILED, SPICY -
and behind every chip a RULE, the whole instruction, written once in this house's voice, so nothing has
to be typed to make it happen. That row is pointed at HIS draft. Nothing in the terminal was pointed at
HER reply: the system stack had a ROLE (who is on the stage) and TRAITS (what she is made of), and
nothing lighter than either. There was no way to say "answer in this key for now" without opening a
block and writing prose into it.

## WHAT IT IS NOW

**A MOOD ROW IN THE SYSTEM PANEL**, under the ROLE row: a chip per mood with the whole rule in its
tooltip, OFF among the chips, DELETE (lit only for a mood written on this device), and **+ NEW MOOD**.

**EIGHT SHIPPED MOODS**, written in `src/js/moods.js` with the other defaults: TENDER, PLAYFUL, HUNGRY,
FERAL, COLD, STILL, GRIEVING, MYTHIC. Every one is a short second-person rule about the KEY and never
about a subject - a mood says how she says it, not what to say.

**THE BLOCK.** `mood` is a real block of the system stack (instr.js's TITLES / ORDER / EDITABLE /
BUILT / NOTES), built each turn from the store by `chat.js`'s `systemParts` and `livePart`, and placed
after the command list and before the two blocks that change every turn - so a mood that stands costs
the prompt cache nothing, and one that is OFF is NOT SENT AT ALL (measured: 676 characters in the
assembled prompt when on, absent when off). It says the colour and whose it is, gives the rule, and then
says what a mood cannot be: not a role, not a trait, not an order about what to say, not something she
owes him - and if the colour does not fit what he actually said, she is told to answer in her own voice
and say so rather than perform it. The bounds, her memory, her role and her voice are all above it. A
colour that could overwrite who she is would not be a colour.

**BOTH PRESENCES CAN WRITE ONE ("I/WE").** He writes from the row, or from the command line
(`/mood HUNGRY :: say what you want plainly`). She writes with a line of her own: `[[MOOD: FERAL]]`
puts on one that exists, `[[MOOD: RAVENOUS :: say plainly what you would eat of him]]` writes a NEW
mood in her own words and puts it on, `[[MOOD: off]]` steps out. Her lane needs no petition, because a
colour is not the machinery she is given - it is hers like a pose or a want - and every one she sets is
printed as a SYSTEM receipt, carried on that turn's archive entry (`mood: [{ mood, by, written }]`), and
in the panel from that moment, where one press takes it off.

## THE "+" OF THE ASK

Worth writing down, because the sentence asked for something to be REINSTATED. The SYSTEM panel already
had `+ NEW BLOCK` (create a block of his own) and `+ NEW ROLE`, and both were photographed in the panel
at 390x844 and 1600x900 while this was built: present, inside the panel, clickable at both sizes.
`+ NEW POSE` is in the forge, `A- / A+` are in the deck. The row this session added has its own
**+ NEW MOOD** - a name and how she is to sound, and the chip is in the row and on her before the row
closes - and that is what "create new ones on the fly" means for moods. If a different `+` has gone
missing somewhere, it is not this panel, and it will get a section of its own the moment he says which
one.

## A BLANK PAGE, FOUND ON THE WAY (AND FIXED)

While the last verification pass of this session was being taken, his own live page came up with
NOTHING on it. `body` carried `doxFull` AND `upperDeckOff` at once, `#output` was `display: none`, the
upper deck `display: none`, and the DOX panel 0px tall inside it - a page with no conversation, no
chrome and no viewer.

The cause is two saved preferences disagreeing, each restored by a different module that never asks the
other: the UPPER DECK had been put away by hand in one session (kv `decks` / `upper: false`) and the
viewer had been left both OPEN and FILLING in another (localStorage `dmsOpen` / `doxFull`). The panel
lives INSIDE the upper deck, so a filled stage whose deck is away has nothing to fill - and the class
that has to stand down is only ever taken off by his hand, by Esc, or by the panel closing.

The rule already existed for a press (decks.js `set()`: putting the upper deck away while the stage is
filled gives the stage back). It now also runs where the preferences are APPLIED, at the end of
`paint()`: if the body is filled and the upper deck's preference is off, `window.dms.fill(false)` - and
`dms.fill` is `setFull` WITHOUT `byHand`, so "selecting the viewer fills it" is not disturbed. Reached
for real, not in theory: the same saved state was reloaded with the guard in place and the page came up
coherent (no `doxFull`, the conversation 205px and back on screen, the upper deck away with its tab and
its reason reading "PUT AWAY BY HAND :: /deck upper on BRINGS IT BACK"). His own state was then put
back the way he left it: the upper deck up, the viewer open and filling, all three localStorage keys
"1".

## STILL OPEN

- **A mood is one colour at a time.** A reply that names two is told so out loud and the second is not
  set - the same rule the page turn uses, and for the same reason: a thing that moves by itself has to
  be accountable for where it is.
- **Nothing fades.** A mood stands until one of them changes it; there is no "for this reply only" and
  no timer. If he ever wants a mood that expires with the turn, that is a different switch and it has
  not been asked for.
